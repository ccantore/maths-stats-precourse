Problem sheet

Instructions

Attempt all ten questions and all 17 scorable parts. The target time is 240 minutes and the total is 100 marks. No calculator or software is needed. Use exact values unless a comparison value is supplied. State supports, parameter restrictions and matrix or vector dimensions when they affect the method.

Use hints.md only after a serious attempt. Do not consult solutions.md until you have completed or paused every part.

Question 1 — membership in a span

Part A — W03-Q01-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Let

𝐯1=[101],𝐯2=[011],𝐰=[2−11]. \mathbf{v}_1= \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{v}_2= \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{w}= \begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix}.

Determine whether 𝐰∈span⁡{𝐯1,𝐯2}\mathbf{w}\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\}. If it is, find the coefficients and verify the linear combination. If it is not, show the inconsistent coefficient condition.

Question 2 — independence, basis and coordinates

Let

𝐮1=[101],𝐮2=[011],𝐮3=[112],𝐮4=[110]. \mathbf{u}_1= \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_2= \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_3= \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\2\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_4= \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}.

Part A — W03-Q02-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Determine whether {𝐮1,𝐮2,𝐮3}\{\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2,\mathbf{u}_3\} is linearly independent. Give an explicit dependence relation and state the dimension of its span.

Part B — W03-Q02-PB (6 marks; 14 minutes)

Show that the ordered set ℬ=(𝐮1,𝐮2,𝐮4)\mathcal{B}=(\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2,\mathbf{u}_4) is a basis of ℝ3\mathbb{R}^3. Then find the coordinate vector [𝐱]ℬ[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} for 𝐱=[3,1,2]𝖳\mathbf{x}=[3,1,2]^{\mathsf T}. Explain why the coordinate vector is unique.

Question 3 — eigenvalues, eigenvectors and a diagonalisation

Let

𝐀=[4123]. \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 4&1\\ 2&3 \end{bmatrix}.

Part A — W03-Q03-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Compute the characteristic polynomial and the eigenvalues of 𝐀\mathbf{A}.

Part B — W03-Q03-PB (7 marks; 16 minutes)

For each eigenvalue, find a corresponding non-zero eigenvector. Construct matrices 𝐏\mathbf{P} and 𝐃\mathbf{D} such that 𝐀=𝐏𝐃𝐏−1\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}. Verify the eigenvector relation in the compact form 𝐀𝐏=𝐏𝐃\mathbf{A}\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}, and state why 𝐏\mathbf{P} is invertible.

Question 4 — diagonalisation and eigenbasis coordinates

Part A — W03-Q04-PA (10 marks; 20 minutes)

Let

𝐁=[3102]. \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 3&1\\ 0&2 \end{bmatrix}.

Diagonalise 𝐁\mathbf{B} over ℝ\mathbb{R}. Let

𝐮=[52]. \mathbf{u}=\begin{bmatrix}5\\2\end{bmatrix}.

Write 𝐮\mathbf{u} in the ordered eigenbasis you use for the diagonalisation. Then use 𝐁=𝐏𝐃𝐏−1\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1} to calculate 𝐁𝐮\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}, and verify the result by direct matrix multiplication. Your derivation must keep eigenvectors and eigenvalues in a consistent order.

Question 5 — a PMF, its CDF and moments

Let XX have support {0,1,2,3}\{0,1,2,3\} and PMF

pX(x)=c(x+1),x∈{0,1,2,3}. p_X(x)=c(x+1), \qquad x\in\{0,1,2,3\}.

Part A — W03-Q05-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Find cc, write the CDF FX(x)F_X(x) for every real xx, and calculate ℙ(1<X≤3)\mathbb{P}(1<X\le3).

Part B — W03-Q05-PB (6 marks; 14 minutes)

Derive 𝔼[X]\mathbb{E}[X] and Var⁡(X)\operatorname{Var}(X). Then let Y=3−2XY=3-2X and calculate 𝔼[Y]\mathbb{E}[Y] and Var⁡(Y)\operatorname{Var}(Y), explaining why the additive constant affects one quantity but not the other.

Question 6 — required discrete distributions

Part A — W03-Q06-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Let K∼Binomial⁡(4,1/2)K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(4,1/2), where KK counts successes in four independent Bernoulli trials, and let G∼Geometric⁡(1/4)G\sim\operatorname{Geometric}(1/4), where GG counts trials up to and including the first success.

Calculate

ℙ(K=2),ℙ(K≥1),ℙ(G>3). \mathbb{P}(K=2),\qquad \mathbb{P}(K\ge1),\qquad \mathbb{P}(G>3).

State the support of each random variable.

Part B — W03-Q06-PB (7 marks; 16 minutes)

Let N∼Poisson⁡(2)N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(2), where 22 is the mean/rate, and let KK be as in Part A. Calculate ℙ(N≤1)\mathbb{P}(N\le1). Then compare the means and variances of NN and KK. Explain how two count distributions can have the same mean but different variances.

Question 7 — required continuous distributions

Part A — W03-Q07-PA (5 marks; 14 minutes)

Let U∼Uniform⁡(−1,3)U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(-1,3). State its support and PDF, write its CDF for every real uu, and calculate ℙ(0<U≤2)\mathbb{P}(0<U\le2).

Part B — W03-Q07-PB (7 marks; 16 minutes)

Let T∼Exponential⁡(2)T\sim\operatorname{Exponential}(2), where 22 is the rate. State its support and PDF, calculate ℙ(T>1)\mathbb{P}(T>1), and find its mean and variance. For Y=4T−1Y=4T-1, calculate 𝔼[Y]\mathbb{E}[Y] and Var⁡(Y)\operatorname{Var}(Y).

Question 8 — covariance, correlation and linear transformations

Part A — W03-Q08-PA (8 marks; 16 minutes)

Random variables XX and YY have finite second moments and satisfy

𝔼[X]=1,𝔼[Y]=2,Var⁡(X)=4,Var⁡(Y)=9,Cov⁡(X,Y)=−3. \mathbb{E}[X]=1,\qquad \mathbb{E}[Y]=2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=4,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y)=9,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=-3.

Calculate Corr⁡(X,Y)\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y). Then define

U=2X−Y,V=X+Y. U=2X-Y, \qquad V=X+Y.

Derive 𝔼[U]\mathbb{E}[U], Var⁡(U)\operatorname{Var}(U), Var⁡(V)\operatorname{Var}(V) and Cov⁡(U,V)\operatorname{Cov}(U,V).

Question 9 — indicator variables and sampling without replacement

Part A — W03-Q09-PA (8 marks; 14 minutes)

Two cards are drawn without replacement from a set of four cards, of which two are red. Let I1I_1 equal one if the first card is red and zero otherwise, and define I2I_2 analogously for the second card. Let R=I1+I2R=I_1+I_2.

Using the indicators, derive 𝔼[R]\mathbb{E}[R], Var⁡(R)\operatorname{Var}(R) and Corr⁡(I1,I2)\operatorname{Corr}(I_1,I_2). Interpret the sign of the correlation in the context of sampling without replacement.

Question 10 — earlier-outcome retrieval

Complete all three parts without consulting Week 1 or Week 2 notes.

Part A — W03-Q10-PA (M4; 3 marks; 10 minutes)

Solve and classify the system

x+y+z=2,2x+2y+2z=4,x−y=0. \begin{aligned} x+y+z&=2,\\ 2x+2y+2z&=4,\\ x-y&=0. \end{aligned}

State the coefficient and augmented ranks and write the complete solution using a real free parameter.

Part B — W03-Q10-PB (S2; 4 marks; 10 minutes)

Events AA and AcA^c form a partition. Suppose

ℙ(A)=13,ℙ(B∣A)=34,ℙ(B∣Ac)=14. \mathbb{P}(A)=\frac13,\qquad \mathbb{P}(B\mid A)=\frac34,\qquad \mathbb{P}(B\mid A^c)=\frac14.

Use total probability and Bayes’ rule to calculate ℙ(A∣B)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B). State the positivity condition needed for the posterior.

Part C — W03-Q10-PC (M2; 4 marks; 10 minutes)

Throughout this part, log\log is the natural logarithm. Solve

log⁡(x−1)−log⁡x=log⁡(13) \log(x-1)-\log x=\log\left(\frac13\right)

over the real numbers. State the domain before combining logarithms and check the final value in the original equation.
