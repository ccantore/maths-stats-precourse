Question 1 — membership in a span

W03-Q01-PA (M5)

Method and conditions. Membership in the span requires real numbers a,ba,b such that a𝐯1+b𝐯2=𝐰a\mathbf{v}_1+b\mathbf{v}_2=\mathbf{w}. The three coordinate equations must be mutually consistent.

The coefficient equation is

a[101]+b[011]=[2−11]. a \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix} +b \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix}.

The first two coordinates give a=2a=2 and b=−1b=-1. The third coordinate requires a+b=1a+b=1, which these values satisfy. Direct verification gives

2𝐯1−𝐯2=[202]−[011]=[2−11]=𝐰. 2\mathbf{v}_1-\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix}2\\0\\2\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix} =\mathbf{w}.

Final result. 𝐰∈span⁡{𝐯1,𝐯2}\mathbf{w}\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\}, with coefficients a=2a=2 and b=−1b=-1.

Marks (5). Correct coefficient equation, 1; first two coordinate conditions, 1; coefficients, 1; consistency check, 1; verified conclusion, 1.

Common mistakes. Comparing only two coordinates; treating the coefficients as vector entries without checking the linear combination; omitting the final consistency check.

Question 2 — independence, basis and coordinates

W03-Q02-PA (M5)

Method and conditions. A non-zero coefficient relation establishes linear dependence. The dimension of the span is the number of vectors in a basis for that span.

The vectors satisfy

𝐮3=𝐮1+𝐮2, \mathbf{u}_3=\mathbf{u}_1+\mathbf{u}_2,

or equivalently,

𝐮1+𝐮2−𝐮3=𝟎. \mathbf{u}_1+\mathbf{u}_2-\mathbf{u}_3=\mathbf{0}.

The coefficients (1,1,−1)(1,1,-1) are not all zero, so the set is linearly dependent.

The two vectors 𝐮1\mathbf{u}_1 and 𝐮2\mathbf{u}_2 are not scalar multiples and are therefore independent. Since 𝐮3\mathbf{u}_3 is already in their span,

span⁡{𝐮1,𝐮2,𝐮3}=span⁡{𝐮1,𝐮2}. \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2,\mathbf{u}_3\} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\}.

Final result. The three-vector set is linearly dependent and its span has dimension 22.

Marks (5). Valid dependence relation, 2; correct dependence conclusion, 1; independence of the remaining pair, 1; dimension conclusion, 1.

Common mistakes. Calling the set independent because none of the vectors is zero; giving a relation whose coefficients are all zero; reporting three as the dimension merely because three vectors were listed.

W03-Q02-PB (M5)

Method and conditions. Three independent vectors in ℝ3\mathbb{R}^3 form a basis. A non-zero determinant tests independence. Coordinates are the coefficients in the ordered basis.

Put the basis candidates into the columns of

𝐏=[101011110]. \mathbf{P} = \begin{bmatrix} 1&0&1\\ 0&1&1\\ 1&1&0 \end{bmatrix}.

Its determinant is

det⁡(𝐏)=1(0−1)+1(0−1)=−2≠0. \det(\mathbf{P}) =1(0-1)+1(0-1) =-2\ne0.

Thus the three columns are independent and form a basis of ℝ3\mathbb{R}^3.

For the coordinates, solve

a𝐮1+b𝐮2+c𝐮4=[312]. a\mathbf{u}_1+b\mathbf{u}_2+c\mathbf{u}_4 = \begin{bmatrix}3\\1\\2\end{bmatrix}.

The coordinate equations are

a+c=3,b+c=1,a+b=2. a+c=3,\qquad b+c=1,\qquad a+b=2.

Adding the first two equations and subtracting the third gives 2c=22c=2, so c=1c=1. Then a=2a=2 and b=0b=0.

Final result and interpretation.

[𝐱]ℬ=[201]. [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} = \begin{bmatrix}2\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}.

The representation is unique because a second coefficient triple would give a non-zero linear dependence relation among the basis vectors after subtraction.

Marks (6). Correct determinant or equivalent independence test, 2; basis conclusion, 1; coefficient equations, 1; coordinates, 1; uniqueness explanation, 1.

Question 5 — a PMF, its CDF and moments
