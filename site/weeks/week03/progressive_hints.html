Week 3 progressive hints

Use the hints in order. After each hint, return to your own work and continue until you reach a new, specific impasse.

W03-Q01-PA

  1. Write a𝐯1+b𝐯2=𝐰a\mathbf{v}_1+b\mathbf{v}_2=\mathbf{w} and compare the three coordinates.
  2. The first two coordinate equations determine aa and bb. Substitute those values into the third coordinate equation before deciding membership.

W03-Q02-PA

  1. Look for a direct linear relation among the three displayed vectors before row-reducing.
  2. Once one vector is written as a combination of the other two, check that the remaining two are not scalar multiples; this determines the dimension of the span.

W03-Q02-PB

  1. Put 𝐮1,𝐮2,𝐮4\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2,\mathbf{u}_4 into the columns of a 3×33\times3 matrix. A non-zero determinant establishes independence.
  2. For the coordinates, solve a𝐮1+b𝐮2+c𝐮4=𝐱a\mathbf{u}_1+b\mathbf{u}_2+c\mathbf{u}_4=\mathbf{x}.
  3. Connect uniqueness of the coefficient triple to linear independence of the ordered basis vectors.

W03-Q03-PA

  1. Form 𝐀−λ𝐈2\mathbf{A}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_2 and set its determinant equal to zero.
  2. Expand the determinant to a quadratic and factor it over the real numbers.

W03-Q03-PB

  1. For each eigenvalue, solve (𝐀−λ𝐈2)𝐯=𝟎(\mathbf{A}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_2)\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0} and choose one non-zero solution.
  2. Put the eigenvectors into the columns of 𝐏\mathbf{P}, and put their eigenvalues into the corresponding diagonal positions of 𝐃\mathbf{D}.
  3. Verify 𝐀𝐏=𝐏𝐃\mathbf{A}\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}. Check det⁡(𝐏)≠0\det(\mathbf{P})\ne0 before using 𝐏−1\mathbf{P}^{-1}.

W03-Q04-PA

  1. The eigenvalues are visible on the diagonal, but the eigenvectors still have to be calculated.
  2. If the columns of 𝐏\mathbf{P} are the ordered eigenvectors, the eigenbasis coordinates of 𝐮\mathbf{u} are 𝐏−1𝐮\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{u}.
  3. Apply 𝐃\mathbf{D} to those coordinates before multiplying by 𝐏\mathbf{P}. Then check the result directly from 𝐁𝐮\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}.

W03-Q05-PA

  1. The four PMF values must sum to one.
  2. Build the CDF by accumulating masses from left to right. Its value changes only at the four support points.
  3. The event 1<X≤31<X\le3 selects support points rather than an interval of continuous mass.

W03-Q05-PB

  1. Calculate 𝔼[X]\mathbb{E}[X] and 𝔼[X2]\mathbb{E}[X^2] from the PMF, then use Var⁡(X)=𝔼[X2]−𝔼[X]2\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mathbb{E}[X]^2.
  2. For constants a,ba,b, 𝔼[a+bX]=a+b𝔼[X]\mathbb{E}[a+bX]=a+b\mathbb{E}[X] and Var⁡(a+bX)=b2Var⁡(X)\operatorname{Var}(a+bX)=b^2\operatorname{Var}(X).
  3. Identify which of these two formulae contains the additive constant.

W03-Q06-PA

  1. Use the binomial PMF (nk)pk(1−p)n−k\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}, and use a complement for ℙ(K≥1)\mathbb{P}(K\ge1).
  2. For the course’s geometric convention, G>3G>3 means that the first three trials are failures.

W03-Q06-PB

  1. For N∼Poisson⁡(λ)N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda), pN(n)=e−λλn/n!p_N(n)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^n/n! on {0,1,2,…}\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.
  2. A Poisson random variable has mean and variance λ\lambda. A binomial random variable has mean npnp and variance np(1−p)np(1-p).
  3. Compare the two means first; equality of means does not force equality of the remaining distributional features.

W03-Q07-PA

  1. A continuous uniform density is constant on its support and integrates to one.
  2. The CDF is zero below the lower endpoint, linear between the endpoints and one above the upper endpoint.
  3. Obtain the requested probability either by integrating the density over the interval or by subtracting two CDF values.

W03-Q07-PB

  1. Under the rate parameterisation, fT(t)=λe−λtf_T(t)=\lambda e^{-\lambda t} for t≥0t\ge0.
  2. The exponential survival probability comes from integrating from the threshold to infinity. Its mean and variance are 1/λ1/\lambda and 1/λ21/\lambda^2.
  3. Apply the expectation and variance rules for the linear transformation, remembering to square its multiplicative coefficient for the variance.

W03-Q08-PA

  1. Correlation divides covariance by the product of the two standard deviations.
  2. Expand variances using Var⁡(aX+bY)=a2Var⁡(X)+b2Var⁡(Y)+2abCov⁡(X,Y)\operatorname{Var}(aX+bY) =a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)+b^2\operatorname{Var}(Y) +2ab\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y).
  3. Expand Cov⁡(2X−Y,X+Y)\operatorname{Cov}(2X-Y,X+Y) bilinearly, then substitute the three supplied second-moment quantities.

W03-Q09-PA

  1. Each IjI_j is Bernoulli with success probability equal to the marginal probability that draw jj is red.
  2. Calculate 𝔼[I1I2]=ℙ(I1=1,I2=1)\mathbb{E}[I_1I_2]=\mathbb{P}(I_1=1,I_2=1) using the two draws without replacement.
  3. Use Var⁡(I1+I2)=Var⁡(I1)+Var⁡(I2)+2Cov⁡(I1,I2)\operatorname{Var}(I_1+I_2) =\operatorname{Var}(I_1)+\operatorname{Var}(I_2) +2\operatorname{Cov}(I_1,I_2), then standardise the covariance to find the correlation.
