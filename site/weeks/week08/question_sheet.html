Instructions

Attempt all 11 questions and all 17 scorable parts. The target time is 240 minutes and the total is 100 marks. No calculator is needed: all critical values, square-root approximations and normal probabilities required for the intended hand solution are supplied.

Show the assumptions or approximation conditions used. Distinguish random variables from observed values and parameters or estimands from estimators and estimates. Round reported interval endpoints, probabilities and test statistics to three decimal places unless an exact expression is more informative.

Question 1 — inferential objects and confidence (S7, S8)

In a population, let p∈(0,1)p\in(0,1) be the proportion of students who complete a preparatory task. In a random sample of n=120n=120 students, let XX be the number who complete it and define

p̂:=X120. \widehat p:=\frac{X}{120}.

The observed count is x=84x=84.

Part A — W08-Q01-PA (5 marks; 10 minutes)

Identify the population parameter, the estimand, the estimator and the observed estimate. Explain why the estimator p̂\widehat p is random before sampling but its realised value 84/12084/120 is not random after the data have been observed.

Part B — W08-Q01-PB (5 marks; 10 minutes)

A 95% confidence-interval method for pp is used repeatedly on independent samples of size 120. Assess both statements:

  1. “For any one realised interval, there is a 95% probability that the fixed parameter pp lies inside it.”
  2. “In repeated sampling, approximately 95% of the intervals constructed by this method cover pp, provided the method’s conditions hold.”

State the valid frequentist interpretation and explain what is random.
