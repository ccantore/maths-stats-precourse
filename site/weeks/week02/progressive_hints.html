Week 2 progressive hints

Use one hint at a time. Return to the question after each hint before reading the next one.

W02-Q01-PA

  1. Check the inner dimensions before multiplying: 𝐀\mathbf{A} is 2×32\times3 and 𝐁\mathbf{B} is 3×23\times2.
  2. Form each entry by taking one row of 𝐀\mathbf{A} times one column of 𝐁\mathbf{B}.
  3. For the associativity check, first find 𝐁𝐮\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}, then compare the resulting vector with the product of 𝐀𝐁\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} and 𝐮\mathbf{u}.

W02-Q01-PB

  1. The reversed product also exists, but its outer dimensions are different.
  2. Each row of 𝐁𝐀\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A} is a linear combination of the two rows of 𝐀\mathbf{A}, with coefficients from the corresponding row of 𝐁\mathbf{B}.
  3. Transpose your 3×33\times3 product. Separately multiply the 3×23\times2 matrix 𝐀𝖳\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T} by the 2×32\times3 matrix 𝐁𝖳\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf T}, then compare both the dimensions and entries.

W02-Q02-PA

  1. For a 2×22\times2 matrix, compute ad−bcad-bc first.
  2. Because the determinant is non-zero, interchange the diagonal entries, change the signs of the off-diagonal entries, and divide by the determinant.
  3. Multiply 𝐂\mathbf{C} by the proposed inverse; the diagonal entries of the product should be one and the off-diagonal entries zero.

W02-Q02-PB

  1. Left-multiply both sides by 𝐂−1\mathbf{C}^{-1}, so 𝐗=𝐂−1𝐃\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{D} for the displayed right-hand-side matrix 𝐃\mathbf{D}.
  2. Treat the two columns of 𝐃\mathbf{D} as two right-hand sides and keep the two solution columns in the same order.
  3. Verify the full matrix product and connect uniqueness to the non-zero determinant.

W02-Q03-PA

  1. Write the 4×54\times5 augmented matrix and use the first row to eliminate xx from rows 2, 3 and 4.
  2. One convenient start is R2←R2−2R1R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1 and R3←R3−R1R_3\leftarrow R_3-R_1, followed by R4←R4−3R1R_4\leftarrow R_4-3R_1.
  3. Swap the new rows 2 and 3 to obtain a pivot of one in the yy column; then eliminate yy, eliminate zz, and back-substitute from ww.

W02-Q04-PA

  1. The coefficient row of the third equation is the sum of the first two coefficient rows.
  2. Use R2←R2−2R1R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1, R3←R3−3R1R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_1, and then subtract the new row 2 from row 3.
  3. The final augmented entry depends on kk. Decide when it is zero.

W02-Q04-PB

  1. Compare rank⁡(𝐀)\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) with rank⁡([𝐀∣𝐛])\operatorname{rank}([\mathbf{A}\mid\mathbf{b}]) before counting free variables.
  2. In the consistent case there are three unknowns and only two pivots. Let z=tz=t.
  3. Use the second echelon equation to find yy, then the first to find xx. Substitute the resulting expressions into each original equation to perform the requested verification.

W02-Q05-PA

  1. To make 𝐏𝐐=𝟎2×2\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{0}_{2\times2}, arrange for every column of 𝐐\mathbf{Q} to be sent to zero by 𝐏\mathbf{P}.
  2. Let 𝐏\mathbf{P} keep the first coordinate and kill the second. Choose a rank-one 𝐐\mathbf{Q} whose non-zero output lies in the second coordinate, but which does not kill the first coordinate.
  3. Compute the reverse product separately; do not infer it from the forward product.

W02-Q06-PA

  1. Use the multiplication rule separately on the branches EE and EcE^c, beginning with ℙ(Ec)\mathbb{P}(E^c).
  2. The conditional probabilities of McM^c are the complements of the supplied conditional probabilities of MM.
  3. Arrange the four joint probabilities in a table and obtain each marginal by summing its row or column.

W02-Q06-PB

  1. Use the MM column total as the denominator of ℙ(E∣M)\mathbb{P}(E\mid M) and the McM^c column total as the denominator of ℙ(E∣Mc)\mathbb{P}(E\mid M^c).
  2. Independence would require the conditional probability of EE to equal its marginal probability in either conditioning group.
  3. Describe the direction of association by comparing the two conditional proportions, without claiming that the context establishes causation.

W02-Q07-PA

  1. The three session events form a partition, so there are three mutually exclusive routes to CC.
  2. Multiply ℙ(C∣Ri)\mathbb{P}(C\mid R_i) and ℙ(Cc∣Ri)\mathbb{P}(C^c\mid R_i) by ℙ(Ri)\mathbb{P}(R_i) to fill each row.
  3. Add the CC column to obtain ℙ(C)\mathbb{P}(C), and check all six cells sum to one.

W02-Q07-PB

  1. The requested conditioning direction is the reverse of the probabilities supplied in the question.
  2. Divide each entry in the CC column by the CC column total.
  3. A valid posterior distribution over the three sessions must sum to one; then compare the three values.

W02-Q08-PA

  1. Recover ℙ(A∩B)\mathbb{P}(A\cap B) from the addition rule for ℙ(A∪B)\mathbb{P}(A\cup B).
  2. Divide the intersection by ℙ(B)\mathbb{P}(B) to find ℙ(A∣B)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B).
  3. Compare the intersection with ℙ(A)ℙ(B)\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(B) and the conditional probability with ℙ(A)\mathbb{P}(A). For disjointness, compare the intersection with zero.

W02-Q09-PA

  1. Treat DD and DcD^c as a partition and find ℙ(S)\mathbb{P}(S).
  2. For the negative signal, complement both conditional signal probabilities and find ℙ(Sc)\mathbb{P}(S^c) before applying Bayes’ rule again.
  3. To interpret the result, distinguish ℙ(S∣D)\mathbb{P}(S\mid D) from ℙ(D∣S)\mathbb{P}(D\mid S), and account for the large DcD^c group.

W02-Q10-PA

  1. Both logarithm arguments must be positive.
  2. Combine the logarithms into the logarithm of a product, then use the fact that log\log is one-to-one on its domain.
  3. Solve the resulting quadratic and test every root against the original domain.

W02-Q10-PB

  1. Evaluate f(1)f(1), f′(1)f'(1) and f″(1)f''(1).
  2. Substitute those values into f(1)+f′(1)(x−1)+12f″(1)(x−1)2f(1)+f'(1)(x-1)+\tfrac12f''(1)(x-1)^2.
  3. The displacements are −0.1-0.1 and 0.10.1. Squaring removes the sign, while f″(1)<0f''(1)<0 fixes the sign of the quadratic correction.

W02-Q10-PC

  1. “At least one” corresponds to a union. Apply De Morgan’s law to write the complement as the event that neither named student is selected.
  2. There are (52)\binom{5}{2} unordered pairs in total. For the complement, choose both representatives from the other three students.
  3. For a direct check, partition the favourable pairs into the disjoint cases “exactly one named student” and “both named students”.
