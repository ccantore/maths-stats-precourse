Problem sheet

Instructions

Attempt all ten questions and all 17 scorable parts. The target time is 240 minutes and the total is 100 marks. No calculator or software is needed. Show row operations, intermediate probabilities and any condition used to justify a classification. Give exact values unless a question says otherwise.

Use hints.md only after a serious attempt. Do not consult solutions.md until you have completed or paused every part.

Question 1: matrix multiplication (M4, 11 marks, 26 minutes)

Let

𝐀=[12−1031],𝐁=[20−1132]. \mathbf{A}= \begin{bmatrix} 1&2&-1\\ 0&3&1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{B}= \begin{bmatrix} 2&0\\ -1&1\\ 3&2 \end{bmatrix}.

Let 𝐮=[1,−1]𝖳\mathbf{u}=[1,-1]^{\mathsf T}.

(a) W02-Q01-PA — 5 marks. Compute 𝐀𝐁\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}, stating the dimension of the product. Then compute both 𝐀(𝐁𝐮)\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}) and (𝐀𝐁)𝐮(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})\mathbf{u} to verify associativity for these matrices and this vector.

(b) W02-Q01-PB — 6 marks. Compute 𝐁𝐀\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}. Verify directly that (𝐁𝐀)𝖳=𝐀𝖳𝐁𝖳(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A})^{\mathsf T} =\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf T}. Use your results and a dimension argument to explain why matrix multiplication is not commutative in this example.

Question 2: determinant, inverse and solution (M4, 12 marks, 26 minutes)

Let

𝐂=[2153]. \mathbf{C}= \begin{bmatrix} 2&1\\ 5&3 \end{bmatrix}.

(a) W02-Q02-PA — 6 marks. Compute det⁡(𝐂)\det(\mathbf{C}) and 𝐂−1\mathbf{C}^{-1}. Verify the inverse by computing 𝐂𝐂−1\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}^{-1}.

(b) W02-Q02-PB — 6 marks. Use the inverse to solve the two systems simultaneously in the matrix equation

𝐂𝐗=[1247], \mathbf{C}\mathbf{X}= \begin{bmatrix} 1&2\\ 4&7 \end{bmatrix},

and verify the result by multiplying 𝐂𝐗\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X}. State why each right-hand side has a unique solution.

Question 3: Gaussian elimination (M4, 10 marks, 22 minutes)

W02-Q03-PA — 10 marks. Use Gaussian elimination to solve

x+y+z+w=5,2x−y+z=−1,x+2y−z+2w=12,3x+y+2z−w=0. \begin{aligned} x+y+z+w&=5,\\ 2x-y+z&=-1,\\ x+2y-z+2w&=12,\\ 3x+y+2z-w&=0. \end{aligned}

Show the row operations and classify the system as having a unique solution, no solution or multiple solutions. Verify your solution in the original four equations.

Question 4: rank and parameter-dependent solvability (M4, 15 marks, 30 minutes)

For k∈ℝk\in\mathbb{R}, consider

x+y+z=1,2x+3y+4z=2,3x+4y+5z=k. \begin{aligned} x+y+z&=1,\\ 2x+3y+4z&=2,\\ 3x+4y+5z&=k. \end{aligned}

Let 𝐀\mathbf{A} be the coefficient matrix and let 𝐛=[1,2,k]𝖳\mathbf{b}=[1,2,k]^{\mathsf T}.

(a) W02-Q04-PA — 7 marks. Row-reduce the augmented matrix far enough to determine rank⁡(𝐀)\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) and rank⁡([𝐀∣𝐛])\operatorname{rank}([\mathbf{A}\mid\mathbf{b}]) when k=3k=3 and when k≠3k\ne3.

(b) W02-Q04-PB — 8 marks. For each case, classify the system and justify the classification using the ranks and the number of unknowns. When k=3k=3, write the solution set using one free parameter and verify that the parameterisation satisfies all three original equations.

Question 5: a rank claim (M4, 10 marks, 18 minutes)

W02-Q05-PA — 10 marks. A student claims that for every pair of 2×22\times2 matrices 𝐏\mathbf{P} and 𝐐\mathbf{Q},

rank⁡(𝐏𝐐)=min⁡{rank⁡(𝐏),rank⁡(𝐐)}. \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}) =\min\{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{P}), \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{Q})\}.

Construct two rank-one 2×22\times2 matrices for which 𝐏𝐐\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q} is the zero matrix but 𝐐𝐏\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P} is non-zero. Compute both products and the ranks of 𝐏\mathbf{P}, 𝐐\mathbf{Q}, 𝐏𝐐\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q} and 𝐐𝐏\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}. Explain both why your example disproves the claim and why the order of the factors matters here.

Question 6: conditioning within a cohort (S2, 11 marks, 27 minutes)

Let EE be the event that a student has an economics background and let MM be the event that the student completed a matrix refresher. Suppose

ℙ(E)=35,ℙ(M∣E)=23,ℙ(M∣Ec)=14. \mathbb{P}(E)=\frac{3}{5}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(M\mid E)=\frac{2}{3}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(M\mid E^c)=\frac{1}{4}.

(a) W02-Q06-PA — 5 marks. Construct the complete 2×22\times2 probability table for the four events E∩ME\cap M, E∩McE\cap M^c, Ec∩ME^c\cap M and Ec∩McE^c\cap M^c. Include the row and column marginal probabilities and check that the four joint probabilities sum to one.

(b) W02-Q06-PB — 6 marks. Compute both ℙ(E∣M)\mathbb{P}(E\mid M) and ℙ(E∣Mc)\mathbb{P}(E\mid M^c). Compare them with ℙ(E)\mathbb{P}(E), determine whether EE and MM are independent, and interpret the direction of the association in one sentence.

Question 7: total probability and Bayes’ rule (S2, 11 marks, 29 minutes)

Students are assigned to review sessions R1R_1, R2R_2 and R3R_3 with probabilities 1/21/2, 1/31/3 and 1/61/6, respectively. These events form a partition of the student group. Let CC be the event that a student submits a correct worksheet. Suppose

ℙ(C∣R1)=34,ℙ(C∣R2)=12,ℙ(C∣R3)=14. \mathbb{P}(C\mid R_1)=\frac{3}{4}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(C\mid R_2)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(C\mid R_3)=\frac{1}{4}.

(a) W02-Q07-PA — 5 marks. Construct the joint probability table with rows R1,R2,R3R_1,R_2,R_3 and columns C,CcC,C^c, including marginal totals. Use the table or the law of total probability to compute ℙ(C)\mathbb{P}(C).

(b) W02-Q07-PB — 6 marks. Given that a student’s worksheet is correct, compute ℙ(Ri∣C)\mathbb{P}(R_i\mid C) for all three sessions. Name the rule used, check that the three posterior probabilities sum to one, and identify the most likely session for a student with a correct worksheet.

Question 8: testing independence (S2, 6 marks, 14 minutes)

W02-Q08-PA — 6 marks. Events AA and BB satisfy

ℙ(A)=12,ℙ(B)=25,ℙ(A∪B)=710. \mathbb{P}(A)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(B)=\frac{2}{5}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(A\cup B)=\frac{7}{10}.

Compute ℙ(A∩B)\mathbb{P}(A\cap B) and ℙ(A∣B)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B). Determine whether AA and BB are independent using two equivalent probability equalities, and state whether the events are disjoint.

Question 9: interpreting a signal (S2, 6 marks, 18 minutes)

W02-Q09-PA — 6 marks. Let DD be an event with ℙ(D)=1/20\mathbb{P}(D)=1/20, and let SS be the event that a signal is positive. Suppose ℙ(S∣D)=9/10\mathbb{P}(S\mid D)=9/10 and ℙ(S∣Dc)=1/10\mathbb{P}(S\mid D^c)=1/10. Compute both ℙ(D∣S)\mathbb{P}(D\mid S) and ℙ(D∣Sc)\mathbb{P}(D\mid S^c) using Bayes’ rule. Compare the two posterior probabilities and explain why ℙ(D∣S)\mathbb{P}(D\mid S) is not 9/109/10.

Question 10: Week 1 retrieval (8 marks, 30 minutes)

These three parts retrieve previously studied material. Complete them without looking back at Week 1 notes.

(a) W02-Q10-PA — M2, 3 marks. Solve

log⁡x+log⁡(x−3)=log⁡4, \log x+\log(x-3)=\log4,

stating the domain restriction and rejecting any inadmissible root.

(b) W02-Q10-PB — M3, 3 marks. Find the second-order Taylor approximation of f(x)=log⁡xf(x)=\log x about x=1x=1, and use it to approximate both log⁡(0.9)\log(0.9) and log⁡(1.1)\log(1.1). Explain why the quadratic correction has the same sign in both approximations.

(c) W02-Q10-PC — M1 and S1, 2 marks. Two representatives are selected uniformly from five students, with order irrelevant. Two of the students are Alice and Benoit. Let AA be the event that Alice is selected and BB the event that Benoit is selected. Express “at least one is selected” and its complement in set notation, using De Morgan’s law. Compute the probability by a complementary counting argument, then confirm it by a direct count that avoids double-counting.
