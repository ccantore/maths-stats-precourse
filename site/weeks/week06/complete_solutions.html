W06-Q01-PA (M9)

Method and conditions. For an unconstrained differentiable objective on ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2, an interior local optimum must be stationary. A definite Hessian at a stationary point supplies a strict local sufficient condition.

The column gradient is

∇f(x,y)=[2x+y−6x+2y]. \nabla f(x,y)= \begin{bmatrix} 2x+y-6\\ x+2y \end{bmatrix}.

The stationary equations are

2x+y=6,x+2y=0. 2x+y=6, \qquad x+2y=0.

They give y=−2y=-2 and x=4x=4. The Hessian is

𝐇f=[2112]. \mathbf{H}_f= \begin{bmatrix} 2&1\\ 1&2 \end{bmatrix}.

Its leading principal minors are 2>02>0 and 4−1=3>04-1=3>0, so it is positive definite.

Final result. The only stationary point is (4,−2)(4,-2). It is a strict local minimum.

Marks (6). Correct column gradient, 1; stationary equations, 1; solution (4,−2)(4,-2), 2; correct Hessian, 1; valid positive-definiteness classification and local conclusion, 1.

Common mistakes. Writing the gradient as a row; solving only one stationary equation; treating the first-order condition as sufficient; misclassifying a positive definite Hessian as a maximum.
