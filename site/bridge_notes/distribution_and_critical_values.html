Distributions and critical values

This sheet is a compact lookup for the probability models used in S3\mathrm{S3} and the sampling and inference methods in S6\mathrm{S6}–S9\mathrm{S9}. A random variable is written with an upper-case letter (for example, XX); xx denotes a realised value. A PMF is written pX(x)p_X(x), a PDF fX(x)f_X(x), and a CDF is FX(x):=ℙ(X≤x)F_X(x):=\mathbb{P}(X\le x). Values outside a stated support have PMF or PDF equal to zero.

How to use the table

  1. Identify whether the variable is discrete or continuous, then check the parameterisation, restrictions and support before substituting numbers.
  2. For a discrete variable, sum the PMF over the required support points. For a continuous variable, integrate the PDF over an interval; a single point has probability zero. Use the CDF for intervals and tails.
  3. A qq-quantile is xq:=inf⁡{x:FX(x)≥q},0<q<1. x_q:=\inf\{x:F_X(x)\ge q\},\qquad 0<q<1. For continuous distributions, FX(xq)=qF_X(x_q)=q. If Z∼𝒩(0,1)Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1), write Φ(z):=ℙ(Z≤z),zq:=Φ−1(q). \Phi(z):=\mathbb{P}(Z\le z),\qquad z_q:=\Phi^{-1}(q). If T∼tνT\sim t_\nu, write tq,ν:=inf⁡{t:ℙ(T≤t)≥q}. t_{q,\nu}:=\inf\{t:\mathbb{P}(T\le t)\ge q\}. Both reference distributions are symmetric, so z1−q=−zqz_{1-q}=-z_q and t1−q,ν=−tq,νt_{1-q,\nu}=-t_{q,\nu}. The tables give positive upper quantiles. A lower-tail critical value is its negative; a two-sided level-α\alpha procedure uses the 1−α/21-\alpha/2 column.

For X∼𝒩(μ,σ2)X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2), let μ∈ℝ\mu\in\mathbb{R} and σ2>0\sigma^2>0, so σ:=σ2>0\sigma:=\sqrt{\sigma^2}>0. The second parameter is the variance. Its support is ℝ\mathbb{R}, its density is

Sampling and inference reminders

Samples, the LLN and the CLT (S6\mathrm{S6})

For an i.i.d. sample with 𝔼[Xi]=μ\mathbb{E}[X_i]=\mu and Var⁡(Xi)=σ2<∞\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\sigma^2<\infty,

𝔼[X‾]=μ,Var⁡(X‾)=σ2n. \mathbb{E}[\bar X]=\mu, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.

The law of large numbers says X‾→ℙμ\bar X\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mu as n→∞n\to\infty (under the stated i.i.d. finite-moment conditions). It is not a claim that observations become normal. If σ2>0\sigma^2>0, the central limit theorem gives the large-nn approximation

X‾−μσ/n≈𝒩(0,1). \frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\ \approx\ \mathcal{N}(0,1).

When the population is normal, X‾∼𝒩(μ,σ2/n)\bar X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2/n) exactly for every nn; otherwise the CLT statement is generally approximate.

Confidence intervals (S8\mathrm{S8})

For a normal population with known σ\sigma, an exact two-sided 100(1−α)%100(1-\alpha)\% interval for μ\mu is

[X‾−z1−α/2σn,X‾+z1−α/2σn]. \left[\bar X-z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}, \bar X+z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\right].

For a normal population with unknown σ2\sigma^2, use SS and the exact Student’s tt pivot:

[X‾−t1−α/2,n−1Sn,X‾+t1−α/2,n−1Sn]. \left[\bar X-t_{1-\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{S}{\sqrt n}, \bar X+t_{1-\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{S}{\sqrt n}\right].

For an iid Bernoulli sample X1,…,XnX_1,\ldots,X_n, let K:=∑i=1nXi∼Binomial⁡(n,p)K:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p) be the success count and let p̂:=K/n=X‾\widehat p:=K/n=\bar X. When np̂n\widehat p and n(1−p̂)n(1-\widehat p) are sufficiently large, the usual large-sample interval is

p̂±z1−α/2p̂(1−p̂)n. \widehat p\ \pm\ z_{1-\alpha/2} \sqrt{\frac{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)}{n}}.

The confidence level describes repeated-sampling coverage when the method’s conditions hold. After data are observed, the realised endpoints are fixed; do not describe them as assigning probability to a fixed parameter.

Tests and errors (S9\mathrm{S9})

For H0:μ=μ0H_0:\mu=\mu_0, use

z=X‾−μ0σ/n(known variance),t=X‾−μ0S/n∼tn−1(normal population, unknown variance). z=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt n} \quad\text{(known variance)},\qquad t=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1} \quad\text{(normal population, unknown variance)}.

For iid observations, the known-variance zz statistic is exactly standard normal under H0H_0 when the population is normal and is approximately standard normal under the finite-variance, large-sample CLT conditions stated above. The tt result is exact under the displayed normal-population condition.

For an iid Bernoulli sample and H0:p=p0H_0:p=p_0, where 0<p0<10<p_0<1, a large-sample proportion test uses

z=p̂−p0p0(1−p0)/n. z=\frac{\widehat p-p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}.

The normal approximation requires both null expected counts np0np_0 and n(1−p0)n(1-p_0) to be sufficiently large.

Choose the tail from H1H_1: reject in an upper tail when the statistic is large, in a lower tail when it is small, and in both tails when H1H_1 is two-sided. The pp-value is the probability under H0H_0 of a result at least as incompatible with H0H_0 as the observed statistic; it is not the probability that H0H_0 is true. A Type I error has probability α\alpha; at a specified alternative θ\theta, Type II error is β(θ)\beta(\theta) and power is 1−β(θ)1-\beta(\theta).

With the same standard error and normal or tt method, a two-sided level- α\alpha test of H0:μ=μ0H_0:\mu=\mu_0 rejects exactly when μ0\mu_0 lies outside the corresponding 100(1−α)%100(1-\alpha)\% confidence interval.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not replace the course geometric variable (trials including the success) by a failures-before-success convention.
  • Do not treat an exponential parameter λ\lambda as a mean, or the second normal parameter σ2\sigma^2 as a standard deviation.
  • A density value is not a probability; integrate a PDF over an interval.
  • Check the tt degrees of freedom (n−1n-1) and the normal-population condition before using an exact small-sample tt interval or test.
  • The LLN does not assert normality, and a pp-value is not ℙ(H0 is true)\mathbb{P}(H_0\text{ is true}). Use S2S^2 with denominator n−1n-1.
