Checkpoint B - question paper

Attempt guide

Checkpoint B is a formative cumulative assessment of selected required material from Weeks 5–7. It contains 40 marks and has a 75-minute time limit. All questions and parts are compulsory.

Student sequence

  1. Open the formula sheet and candidate paper only.
  2. Complete the paper in 75 minutes without notes or solutions.
  3. Use the full solutions to review the attempt and identify revision priorities.
  4. Record the first cause of each lost mark by learning-outcome ID.
  5. Revisit the named Week 5, 6 or 7 material and reattempt missed parts from a blank page after two or three days.

Attempt sequence

  1. Open the question paper and formula sheet.
  2. Complete the paper in 60 minutes without notes or solutions.
  3. Use the full solutions to review the attempt and identify revision priorities.
  4. Record the first cause of each lost mark and schedule a blank-page reattempt after two or three days.

Conditions

A basic non-programmable scientific calculator is permitted, although no question requires one. Software, programmable calculators, computer algebra and statistical packages are not permitted. The checkpoint contains no optional dynamics, regression or other excluded content.

Checkpoint B is diagnostic and not pass/fail. Use the evidence from the attempt to guide revision and reattempts. For this controlled first-cohort pilot, scores are not used for admission, progression, ranking, funding or grading.

General conventions

  • Give exact answers unless a question asks for an approximation.
  • All vectors and gradients are column vectors.
  • For a maximisation problem, write inequalities as gj(𝐱)≥0g_j(\mathbf{x})\ge0 and use the course Kuhn–Tucker sign convention stated in the course materials.
  • For a random sample, X‾=n−1∑i=1nXi\bar X=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i.
  • Φ\Phi denotes the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

Formula provision

The geometric PMF, support and mean needed in Question 7 and the numerical normal CDF value needed in Question 6 are supplied in the candidate paper. No additional formulae or numerical tables are supplied because the definiteness, optimisation, conditioning, sampling and estimation identities used in the questions are part of the assessed material.
