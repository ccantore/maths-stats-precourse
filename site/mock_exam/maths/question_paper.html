Mock diagnostic examination - Maths paper

Candidate paper

Time allowed: 90 minutes
Marks for this paper: 55
Marks across both mock papers: 100
Questions: All questions and all parts are compulsory.

Instructions

  • Complete this paper independently of the Statistics paper.
  • Complete the paper without notes or solutions. You may use the supplied Maths formula sheet.
  • A basic non-programmable scientific calculator is permitted, but no question requires one.
  • No computational software is needed or permitted. Approved assistive technology, accessibility accommodations and adjusted timing remain permitted where authorised; assistive tools must not perform the assessed mathematics.
  • Show the reasoning and intermediate work needed to support each answer. An unsupported answer may not receive full marks.
  • Give exact answers unless a question asks for an approximation.
  • All vectors are column vectors. Use the course notation throughout.
  • For inequality-constrained maximisation, write every inequality as gj(𝐱)≥0g_j(\mathbf{x})\geq 0 and use the course Kuhn–Tucker convention.

Section M0 — short diagnostic questions (10 marks; 15 minutes)

Question 1 — MOCK-M0-Q01-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Consider the claim

∀x∈ℝ,x2≥x. \forall x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad x^2\geq x.

Write its logical negation using ∃\exists, then give one counterexample to the claim.

Question 2 — MOCK-M0-Q02-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Solve

log⁡(x−1)+log⁡(x+1)=log⁡8 \log(x-1)+\log(x+1)=\log 8

over its real domain.

Question 3 — MOCK-M0-Q03-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Find the first-order Taylor approximation to f(x)=xf(x)=\sqrt{x} about x=4x=4, and use it to approximate 4.4\sqrt{4.4}.

Question 4 — MOCK-M0-Q04-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Let 𝐅:ℝ3→ℝ2\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2 and h:ℝ2→ℝh:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R} be differentiable. State the dimensions of 𝐉𝐅(𝐱)\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{x}) and of the column gradient ∇h(𝐮)\nabla h(\mathbf{u}).

Question 5 — MOCK-M0-Q05-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Classify the quadratic form

q(x,y)=2x2+2xy+2y2 q(x,y)=2x^2+2xy+2y^2

by definiteness. Name the test or calculation that supports your conclusion.

Section M1 — linear algebra (15 marks; 25 minutes)

Question 6 — systems, rank and parameter cases (8 marks; 14 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M1-Q06-PA

For parameters a,b∈ℝa,b\in\mathbb{R}, consider

x+y+z=1,x+ay+z=b,x+y+az=b. \begin{aligned} x+y+z&=1,\\ x+ay+z&=b,\\ x+y+az&=b. \end{aligned}

Use Gaussian elimination to classify the system for every pair (a,b)(a,b) as having a unique solution, no solution or multiple solutions. Give the unique solution when it exists. When a=1a=1, state both the coefficient rank and the augmented-matrix rank for each possible case.

Question 7 — eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalisation (7 marks; 11 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M1-Q07-PA

Let

𝐀=[4101]. \mathbf{A}= \begin{bmatrix} 4&1\\ 0&1 \end{bmatrix}.

Find the eigenvalues and a basis for each eigenspace. Explain why the chosen eigenvectors form a basis of ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2, then give matrices 𝐏\mathbf{P} and 𝐃\mathbf{D} such that 𝐀=𝐏𝐃𝐏−1\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}.

Section M2 — multivariable calculus (12 marks; 20 minutes)

Question 8 — a vector chain rule (4 marks; 7 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M2-Q08-PA

Define 𝐅:ℝ2→ℝ2\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2 and 𝐆:ℝ3→ℝ2\mathbf{G}:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2 by

𝐅(u,v)=[u2+vuv],𝐆(x,y,z)=[x+yyz]. \mathbf{F}(u,v)= \begin{bmatrix} u^2+v\\ uv \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{G}(x,y,z)= \begin{bmatrix} x+y\\ yz \end{bmatrix}.

Use the multivariable chain rule to calculate 𝐉𝐅∘𝐆(1,1,1)\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}\circ\mathbf{G}}(1,1,1). Show the dimensions of the two Jacobian matrices before multiplying them.

Question 9 — implicit differentiation (4 marks; 7 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M2-Q09-PA

The equation

F(x,y):=x2+xy+y2−7=0 F(x,y):=x^2+xy+y^2-7=0

holds at (x,y)=(1,2)(x,y)=(1,2). State the derivative condition needed for this equation to define yy locally as a differentiable function of xx. Calculate dy/dx\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}x at (1,2)(1,2), then use it to approximate the change in yy when xx increases by 0.050.05.

Question 10 — homogeneity and Euler’s theorem (4 marks; 6 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M2-Q10-PA

For x>0x>0 and y>0y>0, define

p(x,y)=x2/5y3/5. p(x,y)=x^{2/5}y^{3/5}.

Verify from the definition that pp is homogeneous and state its degree. Use Euler’s theorem to obtain a relation between pp, pxp_x and pyp_y, and interpret what happens to pp when both inputs are doubled.

Section M3 — curvature and optimisation (18 marks; 30 minutes)

Question 11 — unconstrained optimisation (6 marks; 10 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M3-Q11-PA

Let

f(x,y)=4x+6y−x2−xy−y2,(x,y)∈ℝ2. f(x,y)=4x+6y-x^2-xy-y^2, \qquad (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2.

Find every stationary point and classify it using the Hessian. State the condition that makes your conclusion global on ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2, then give the global optimum value.

Question 12 — equality-constrained optimisation and the envelope theorem (6 marks; 10 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M3-Q12-PA

For a resource parameter a>0a>0, consider

max⁡x>0,y>0{2log⁡x+log⁡y}subject to2x+y=a. \max_{x>0,\,y>0}\{2\log x+\log y\} \quad\text{subject to}\quad 2x+y=a.

Encode the constraint as a−2x−y=0a-2x-y=0. Form the Lagrangian and solve for x*(a)x^*(a), y*(a)y^*(a) and the multiplier μ*(a)\mu^*(a). State a condition that makes the solution the unique global maximiser. Use the envelope theorem to calculate V′(a)V'(a), where VV is the value function, and interpret the sign of the multiplier under the stated constraint encoding.

Question 13 — inequality constraints and a binding resource limit (6 marks; 10 minutes)

Part A — MOCK-M3-Q13-PA

Consider

max⁡x,y{3x+2y−12x2−y2} \max_{x,y}\left\{3x+2y-\frac12x^2-y^2\right\}

subject to

x≥0,y≥0,2−x−y≥0. x\geq0, \qquad y\geq0, \qquad 2-x-y\geq0.

Form the Lagrangian and write the complete Kuhn–Tucker conditions under the course convention. Solve the problem, reporting all multipliers and identifying every active and slack constraint. Use curvature and the feasible set to justify whether the solution is the unique global maximiser.

General conventions

  • Give exact answers unless a question asks for an approximation.
  • Scalars use italic lower-case letters. Vectors are bold lower-case column vectors, and matrices are bold upper-case letters.
  • Use 𝐀𝖳\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T}, det⁡(𝐀)\det(\mathbf{A}), rank⁡(𝐀)\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) and [𝐀∣𝐛][\mathbf{A}\mid\mathbf{b}] for transpose, determinant, rank and an augmented matrix.
  • Gradients are column vectors. For 𝐅:ℝn→ℝm\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m, use 𝐉𝐅\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}} for its Jacobian.
  • For a maximisation problem with inequality constraints, write each inequality as gj(𝐱)≥0g_j(\mathbf{x})\geq0 and use the course Kuhn–Tucker convention.

Formula provision

No additional formulae are supplied. The derivative rules, Taylor formula, matrix tests, chain rule, implicit-differentiation rule, Euler relation, optimisation conditions and Kuhn–Tucker conditions used in this paper are part of the assessed material.
