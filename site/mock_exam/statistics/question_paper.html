Mock diagnostic examination - Probability and Statistics paper

Candidate paper

Time allowed: 90 minutes
Marks for this paper: 45
Marks across both mock papers: 100
Questions: All questions and all parts are compulsory.

Instructions

  • Complete this paper independently of the Maths paper.
  • Complete the paper without notes or solutions. You may use the supplied Probability and Statistics formula sheet.
  • A basic non-programmable scientific calculator is permitted.
  • No computational or statistical software is needed or permitted. Approved assistive technology, accessibility accommodations and adjusted timing remain permitted where authorised; assistive tools must not perform the assessed probability or statistics.
  • Show the reasoning and intermediate work needed to support each answer. An unsupported answer may not receive full marks.
  • Give exact answers unless the question asks for an approximation. Round requested decimal answers to four decimal places unless stated otherwise.
  • State relevant supports, parameter restrictions and conditions when they affect a method or conclusion.

Section S0 — short diagnostic questions (10 marks; 15 minutes)

Question 1 — MOCK-S0-Q01-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

A two-person committee is selected from five economists and three statisticians. How many different committees contain exactly one economist and one statistician?

Question 2 — MOCK-S0-Q02-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Let X∼Exponential⁡(2)X\sim\operatorname{Exponential}(2), where 22 is the rate. State the support of XX and calculate ℙ(X>1)\mathbb{P}(X>1) exactly.

Question 3 — MOCK-S0-Q03-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Let X1,…,X16X_1,\ldots,X_{16} be iid with

Calculate 𝔼[X‾]\mathbb{E}[\bar X] and Var⁡(X‾)\operatorname{Var}(\bar X).

Question 4 — MOCK-S0-Q04-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

Let

and let λ̂:=X‾\widehat\lambda:=\bar X. For the observed sample (1,0,2,1)(1,0,2,1), identify the parameter, the estimator and the numerical estimate.

Question 5 — MOCK-S0-Q05-PA (2 marks; 3 minutes)

General conventions

  • Random variables use upper-case letters and their realisations use lower-case letters.

  • Use pX(x)p_X(x) for a PMF, fX(x)f_X(x) for a PDF and FX(x)=ℙ(X≤x)F_X(x)=\mathbb{P}(X\leq x) for a CDF.

  • The normal distribution is parameterised as 𝒩(μ,σ2)\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2), where the second parameter is the variance.

  • The exponential parameter λ\lambda is a rate, and a geometric random variable counts trials up to and including the first success.

  • For a random sample,

    X‾=1n∑i=1nXi,S2=1n−1∑i=1n(Xi−X‾)2. \bar X=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i, \qquad S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i-\bar X)^2.

One-sample Student’s t procedure

For an iid sample from a normal population with unknown variance, the two-sided (1−α)100%(1-\alpha)100\% confidence interval for μ\mu is

X‾±t1−α/2,n−1Sn. \bar X\pm t_{1-\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{S}{\sqrt n}.

For testing H0:μ=μ0H_0:\mu=\mu_0, the test statistic is

T=X‾−μ0S/n, T=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{S/\sqrt n},

which has a tn−1t_{n-1} distribution under H0H_0 when the stated conditions hold.
